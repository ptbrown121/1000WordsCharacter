System Concept—Dice 
In 1KW, you will build dice pools before you roll and often 
select the highest among them for your best result.  
However, some functions require extra dice to get a 
good result and/or sustain your resources.  Advancing 
stats and tiles requires finding a balance between high 
dice and more dice.

Character—Part 2: Tiles
The character also builds a set of tiles to form their mosaic.
 
Trait tiles describes the hero’s appearance, behavior, or demeanor.
Story tiles relate a feature of the hero’s race, past, or occupation.
 
Skill tiles come from the basic skills list (next page), the auxiliary 
skills from the archetype lists, or can be made up by the player.
Gear tiles are weapons, tools, spells, tricks, and clothing.
Each tile costs 1XP and begins with a descriptive term or phrase,
 two colors representing stats from the rosette,
 and a d4.
Tiles are advanced using the same dynamic used for stats.
 They may not start with more than 3▟ on any one tile.
Characters begin with 50 XP to build tiles.
Starting characters should aim for 15 tiles.
 More grants endurance but sacrifices power.
 Fewer grants showy success but quick exhaustion.
It isn’t necessary to have all the tiles built to start play.
You can save XP and add tiles as you flesh out your character.
Interviews on the following pages will help with each type.

Character—Part 4: Resource Pools
Each tile gained also improves Resource Pools:
Health:   1 point for each red tile + 1 point for each orange tile.
 This pool records vitality.
 It is often reduced during combat.
Energy:  1 point for each green tile + 1 point for each yellow tile.
 This pool represents drive and willpower
  as much as it covers stamina.
 It can be spent to add an extra die to a pool.
Reflex:   1 point for each purple tile + 1 point for each blue tile.
 This pool represents quickness in movement
  as well as their sharpness of perception.
 It can be spent for Initiative.
Note: Each tile counts twice.
 A tile may add 2 points to the same pool.
 e.g., a yellow and green tile adds 2 points to Energy.
Pools are adjusted whenever new tiles are gained.
 Tiles can be lost temporarily (rarely permanently).
 If a tile is buried, its contributions to pools are lost.
 More about this condition later.

System Concept—Tags
Tiles can also be tailored by adding Tags.
 Most cost 2 to 4 XP to add to a tile.
A tile can have up to 1 tag per ▟,
 although there are a few exceptions.
Build Tags—these add to Skill, Trait, or Story tiles
 and reflect elements of the character’s composition.
 They usually modify pools or specific dice checks.
Detail Tags—these add to Gear tiles
 and reflect elements of equipment composition.
 Some Build tags also appear in the Detail tag list.
 These tags apply only when the tool is used.
Range and Duration Tags—these add to Gear tiles
 and shape the scope of effects done by tools.
 These tags do not count against the ▟ limit.
Crit Tags—these add to Gear tiles
 and modify the kind of impact gets inflicted by the tool.
 Most of them improve the injury dealt to the target.
Shield Tags—these add to Gear tiles
 and modify the kind of protection the tool provides its user.
 Most of them deflect injury dealt to the user.

 Melee: tools that don’t leave the hand during use.

Equipment
Gear tiles have several components.
Detail tags express the gear’s function, durability, and benefits.
 These include some Flaw tags, which can reduce cost
 and model unfamiliarity with new tools.
Weapon categories are Melee or Ranged weapons,
 and weapon power separates Near and Far ranges.
Weapons list Crit tags to define the special injury they inflict.
Some tools (especially spells) also need to describe Duration,
 how long the tool’s effect lasts.
Armor categories are based on material and coverage.
 Soft armors are flexible; Hard armors are rigid.
 Open armors cover a fraction of the body,
 where Full and Closed cover progressively more.
Armor bears Shield tags, listing the Crit tags it soaks.

 for starting characters.

System Concept—Ranges
Linear Range
 Uses a 120° forward arc,
 or a third of a circle.
Touch, Close, and Reach are Melee Range.
Short to Extreme are for Ranged Combat.
Visual is for sightlines.
Earshot is for audible effects.
 These will be situational.
Area and Volume
Explosive effects fill space.
The Zone enlarges the arc to a circular area.
 A Bang fills the Close-range circle.
 A Boom filles the Reach-range circle.
 By itself, a Zone centers on the user.
A Dome enlarges the arc into a hemispheric volume.
Range to Target
Complex ranges can combine for thrown bursts.
 e.g., grenades are Medium / Bang.
 Many spells use this mechanic.
The Linear Range is listed first
 and sets the maximum distance to target
 within the forward arc.
The Area or Volume is listed second
 and sets the size of the affected space.

Improvement
Gaining XP
Play gains the character experience points (XP).
XP are stored until used.
At rest points in the story, players may spend XP.
 They can buy new tiles.
  Remember to update resource pools!
 They can improve existing tiles by
  improving dice,
  adding Detail, Build tags,
  improving Range or Duration tags,
  adding Crit or Shield tags,
  or removing Flaw tags.
 Note that a tile can only have 1 tag per ▟.
  Flaw, Range, or Duration tags don’t count.
Costs for tile improvement are the same as for creating them.
 
Duplication
Each duplicate tag costs 2 XP more than the previous copy.
Copying a tile is the same cost as the original.
Story Point
Stats advance just like tiles, but with one tiny exception.
The PC must do something important.  Meaningful.  Epic.
 The GM awards a Story Point after a significant event.
 Raising the stat costs both XP and a Story Point.
I Dropped My Awesome 23 XP Thingamajig Down a Hole
If your gear is lost or destroyed, its tile is Buried.

All Tags on a Buried tile are out of play.
 e.g., Tough on that tile does NOT provide HP.
The tile and its XP are not lost.
If the item is replaced, even by something close,
  the tile returns to play.

Combat Turns and the Initiative
Brawls slow time to see who does what to whom.
Time is cut up into turns of roughly 10 seconds.
Each participant acts whenever they gain the initiative.
 
The instigator is the first to gain the initiative.
After acting, the instigator passes initiative to another participant.
 The initiative can be given to an ally,
 a member of the opposing faction(s),
 or the stage (i.e., the surroundings).
 Foes and the setting are under the GM’s control.
Initiative is passed around until everyone has had their turn.
The last actor’s handoff starts the next turn.
If a character’s check goes haywire (more than half the dice roll 1s)
 that character cannot pass initiative to an ally.

The Move
Use the Move to travel.
 Distances are usually in yards/meters (Y/M),
 but some battlefields might require an adjustment.
Obstacles, barriers, climbs, and foes may reduce any travel.
A Pace is a short move covering 3 Y/M.
 It can be done once a turn instead of a Shout.
A Walk is a typical Move, equal to current Reflex Y/M.
 It ends at Reach Range.
  
To Run, use both your Action and Move for 3x Walk.
 It is travel to Short range.
To Dash, test Athletics or a similar skill vs. Difficulty 6.
 Minimum travel is 3x Walk.
 Assign a spare die to increase the distance traveled.
 e.g., a spare d6 showing a 4 gives 4 extra Walks.
Take a SLOW Crit after Dashing.

What Can I Do On My Initiative?
A PC’s turn includes three possible components.
 Action—an initiated event using a dice pool.
  Often, this will be an attack check.
 Move—covering ground.
 Shout—a quick dialogue or instruction.
These can be done in any order.
Players always assemble and roll a dice pool.
 On Action, they choose the event.
 On Response, they choose the defense.
The Shout
This is a simple communication action,
 or an adjustment step (a Pace).
It allows for a bit of dialogue between characters,
 but only a few lines.  Allies may respond.
The Action
This is an initiated dice check.
 Typically, it is an attack on an opponent,
 but it could also be a skill check,
  such as defusing a bomb,
  disarming a trap,
  haggling with a merchant, etc.
Defense does not take your action.
 Defense is a response check.
The check can be a Call or Burn.

Pressing the Initiative
Characters can Press the Initiative by spending Reflex.
After the start of combat, characters may
 spend 1 RX to gain a Move, or
 spend 2 RX to gain an Action.
Repeating your previous action (even if it failed the first time)

Taking Injury—Soak, then Grit, then Crits
After confirming that your character has been hit,
 start with Soak and Shield,
 then check Grit and reduce HP,
 then take Crits.
Most new characters will have 0 Soak.
 It comes from Full or Closed armor,
 or the Ironclad tag.
  If it is part of armor,
  the Soak is applied only if the tile is called.
Soak reduces incoming impact.
 It reduces the HP dealt by the incoming attack,
  and it applies before Grit.
 Reducing HP loss to 0 does not stop the hit.
Note that Piercing reduces Soak effectiveness on the target.
Armor also has Shield tags.
 Each Shield tag reduces a matching incoming Crit.
Apply unsoaked harm as HP loss.
Next, check Grit.
If Grit is higher or equal to the HP dealt, Crits are prevented.
If Grit is lower, whatever Crits are left after Shield tags apply.

System Concept—All the Ranges
Linear Range
 Uses a 120° forward arc,
 or a third of a circle.
Close Range equals the Pace.
Reach Range equals the Walk,
 or 1x current RX.
Short Range equals the Run.
Medium Range equals the Dash.
Area and Volume
The Zone enlarges the arc to a circular area.
The Dome enlarges the arc to a half-sphere space.
Crowd
Sometimes it’s not the space,
 it’s the people or creatures in the space.
The listed number counts the nearest X targets
 at the time of the event.
Duration
Spells also consider Time.
Note that spells that create effects produce it,
 but may not determine how long it lasts.

Shadow Play—Qi and Id
In some realms, magic is linked to a struggle between good and evil,
 honor and corruption, light and dark, or order and chaos.
 Magic and its power stem from gods, fate, or ancient forces.
For these worlds, there are two Shadow stats available: Qi and Id.
To improve Qi or Id, XP is spent to raise the higher of the pair.
 This is necessary because the stat values change during play.
Any tile can be built with a Qi or Id color box
 instead of one of the rosette rainbow.
 Qi’s color is White. 
 Id’s color is Black. 
Both contribute to the Shadow Resource pool.
Note that having the Shadow Resource pool lowers your other pools!
Whenever you invoke a Shadow stat to cast,
 1▟ of its value slides to the other stat.
 The lowest either can go is d3.
The difference in ▟ is called aberration.
Characters with high aberration may begin to experience side-effects.

Shadow as a Resource
The Shadow Resource pool is equal to the total
 of White and Black boxes on a character’s tiles.
The pool can be spent as if it were any other resource,
 including taking injury, pressing for initiative, etc.
 It does not improve references, e.g., Walk distance.
If a freebie die is bought with Shadow, maximize that die.
Reduce spell difficulty by Shadow for 1 point.
Increase spell impact by Shadow for 1 point.
Recovering Shadow
Half of the shadowcaster’s maximum points recovers at dawn,
 the other half at dusk.
In some situations, dawn and dusk must be simulated,
 or might be interpreted as falling asleep and waking.

Shadow Tags
A tile with Qi can gain the Light Build/Detail tag.
When a Light tile is called, the slide shifts ▟ toward Qi.
 If it cannot slide (Id is already minimum d3),
 it causes an Aberration check.
Tiles with Id can have the Night Build/Detail tag,
 which drives the slide toward Id.
 If the slide is prevented (Qi is already minimum d3),
 it causes an Aberration check.
Tiles with Qi or Id can have the Gloam Build/Detail tag
This tag drives slide toward balance, an even split between Qi and Id.
 If the slide is prevented (Qi and Id are equal),
 it heals a Shadow point.
Aberration
If the roll causing an Aberration check was Haywire,
 the character displays visual symptoms of its alignment.
This transformation is up to the GM,
 but could be haloes, auras, and feathers for Light
 and horns, cinereous skin, and hooves for Night.
More crucially, the Aberration check is a Footfall
 that draws others of the opposite alignment
 who might be spoiling for a fight.
Tiles describing Aberration probably come from Stranger Celestials,
 and begin with a Hitch.

Shapeshifting and Were-creatures
Small parts of a Bestial Stranger’s nature, e.g. fangs or claws,
 can be concealed until needed.
A hidden natural armament can use the Ambush Build tag,
 which reduces the target’s first defense to zero
 after the tool’s deployment.
More complete form changes use Shape to control a change,
 and benefit from the While X Build tag.
The While X tag is -4 XP,
The X identifies the shape that can use the tile,
 e.g. While Werewolf.
The tile is in play whenever the character is a Werewolf,
 and buried when the Wolf is dormant.
Abilities that the Werewolf cannot use can be tagged in reverse,
 such that While Human tiles get buried
 whenever the wolf comes out to play.
While X cannot be used as a Detail tag on ordinary gear.
While X can also work for gelatinous aliens and xenomorphs,
 morphing robots,
 and transforming superheroes.
If the While X tag is paid off, the feature is evident to observers
but may become a fusion of the two originally separate shapes.

Cyber Dawn—The Era of the Cyborg
The earliest phase of mechanical replacement is cybernetic repair.
 Equipment is mostly physical enhancement.
Cyborgs contend with badly attached hardware (Torn),
 and/or error-prone software (Glitch).
Equipment is hard to interface with (Feedback)
 and may cause negative reactions (Stigma).
Cyber Morning—The Era of the Robot
Robots are not yet complex thinkers (Rube, Overload)
 and/or may have limited emotional range (Numb).
 They are often considered lesser people (Stigma)
 or used as disposable laborers or warriors (Bound).
Cybernetic replacement can strip away privacy (Adware),
 or control though implants (Hacked, Malware).
The line between living and mechanical begins to blur.
Cyber Noon—The Singularity Event
Machines can think, and the machine-mind barrier has been broken,
 but problems for machines (Glitch, Adware, Malware)
 appear in live minds, which can now be edited (Hacked).
Robots reach full consciousness
 but still fight for autonomy (Stigma, Bound).
Solitude and lack of connection become alien (Numb, Solo),
 but it is also possible to get too much data (Overload).
Cyber Eve—The Era of the Norg
The line separating man from machine is erased,
 and some synthetic constructs may be organic.
Robots diversify into Stranger configurations (Undroid),
 but may have draining components (Hungry).
Norgs are minds that are crafted whole, not born.
 They can be put into any body, living or machine.
 Childhood ends.  It takes too long.
Transplanting still requires adjustment (Feedback),
 but can produce AIs in living frames.

Gizmo
This Detail tag converts a Spell into an independent tool.
 Each Gizmo has ▟ HP and may Break on a Haywire roll,
 but it does not need Arcane skills or spell tests.
 Gizmos cannot use Qi or Id.
Gizmo tiles must feature the referred color on the list below,
 and may Chain to an ordinary skill tile with that color.
If it does not Chain a skill, Gizmo only costs 2 XP.

Sliver
This Build tag grafts a Spell into the body as an ability,
 e.g., spit poison, breathe fire, boost Speed, etc.
As a Knack (Silver only), the ability chains to a skill tile

Ammo 
Ammo tiles add potency during combat,
 but also have application in crafting.
They augment the dice pool, often when a Gear tile is called.
They have no dice of their own.
To activate an Ammo tile, the Target must be in use.
Assign a spare die from your pool to the Ammo.
 The Ammo tile replaces the Reload flaw on a firearm.
If the devoted die equals or beats the Ammo Supply (🞧),
 retain the Ammo tile after this action resolves.
Otherwise, it is buried (e.g., the Ammo runs out until replaced).
Refined ammunition for weapons is the basic concept of 
Ammo tiles, but they have wider application.
